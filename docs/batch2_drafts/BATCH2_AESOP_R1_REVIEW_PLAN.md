# Batch 2 Aesop R1 Review Plan

Batch ID: `BATCH2-AESOP-R1-DRAFT-2026-07-08`

Status: **Draft plan; not ready for external family review**

## Why this batch

These five Aesopic stories are short, familiar, and lower risk than Grimm,
Andersen, and longer world-tale adaptations. They are useful for refining the
Teret Bet read-aloud voice before the remaining catalogue is repaired at scale.
This batch is not a rule that future batches should prefer short stories. After
the read-aloud voice is steadier, later batches should deliberately include
mission-significant Ethiopian, African, and major world stories even when they
need more pages and heavier review.

## Common adaptation decisions

- Keep the stories animal-led and simple.
- Avoid lecturing or shaming the character who makes a mistake.
- Use gentle repeated phrases where useful.
- Replace harsh or violent source endings with age-safe consequences.
- Keep the moral visible through the action before stating it.
- Treat the listed draft page count as provisional; add pages if a story beat
  feels cramped during owner or family read-aloud review.
- Localize visually only at the illustration-planning stage; do not claim
  Ethiopian origin for these imported fables.

## Story-specific notes

### The Fox and the Grapes

- Source issue: the classic “sour grapes” ending can feel like mockery.
- Draft approach: the fox first covers his disappointment, then admits the true
  feeling.
- Review question: does this preserve the fable while teaching emotional
  honesty?

### The Tortoise and the Hare

- Source issue: the hare can become a humiliation target.
- Draft approach: the hare boasts, makes a poor choice, then rejoins as a
  friend.
- Review question: does the ending feel warm enough for ages 3-6?

### The Crow and the Pitcher

- Source issue: very compressed action in the classic version.
- Draft approach: give the crow time to observe, try, think, and repeat the
  pebble action.
- Review question: do younger children understand why the water rises?

### The Dog and His Reflection

- Source issue: “greed loses all” can feel punitive, and reflection may be
  abstract for younger children.
- Draft approach: make the stream calm, the dog surprised, and the lesson about
  pausing before grabbing.
- Review question: is the reflection beat clear enough without extra
  explanation?

### The Goose That Laid the Golden Eggs

- Source issue: the traditional killing/cutting-open ending is not suitable for
  Teret Bet's age and safety posture.
- Draft approach: the rushed, impatient farmer frightens the goose away; the
  daily gift ends without violence.
- Review question: does the softened ending still communicate patience and care?
