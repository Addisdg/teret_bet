# Five-Story Pilot Research

Date: 2026-07-02

This is a provenance, rights, cultural-context, and age-fit screen. It does not
approve final story wording, page flow, catalogue replacement, or artwork.

Owner approved the five pilot selections and the three provisional replacement
slots on 2026-07-02. Text remains draft until explicit language and read-aloud
approval.

## Research rules

- Treat the named storyteller/collection record as evidence of the collected
  Ethiopian version, not proof that a motif occurs only in that community.
- Prefer independent corroboration, but never manufacture it when only
  derivative copies of the same collection are available.
- Retell traditional motifs in new Amharic and English wording.
- Do not copy the Ethiopian Folktales website's wording or artwork without
  permission.
- A CC-licensed adaptation may be used only under its exact attribution and
  licence requirements.
- Page count remains open until both complete adaptations pass read-aloud and
  continuity review. New pages receive matching image specifications.

## 1. The Mouse Deer Who Was Cold at Noon

**Collected origin:** Wolayita Zone. Narrated by Yisihak Aldade in the Ethiopian
Story Collecting Project.

**Primary record:**
https://ethiopianfolktales.com/stories/the-mouse-deer-who-was-cold-at-noon/

**Corroboration:** No genuinely independent oral or scholarly record was located
in this pass. Search results repeat the Story Collecting Project account. The
named-storyteller record remains a credible primary basis, but the gap stays
visible for cultural review.

**Rights:** Retell the traditional motif in original words. Do not copy the
project's English/Amharic expression, exercises, or images.

**Age-fit screen:** Promising for ages 3-4. Remove insulting language and avoid
presenting the animal's pretend preparation as literal science. Preserve the
gentle visual joke and forward-planning theme.

**Recommendation:** Advance to `Researched`, conditional on owner/Amharic
reviewer confirmation that the core joke feels natural rather than confusing.

## 2. The Cub and the Kid

**Collected origin:** Amhara. Narrated by Magabi Enyew Gessesse in the Ethiopian
Story Collecting Project; the project record indicates original Amharic audio.

**Primary record:**
https://ethiopianfolktales.com/stories/the-cub-and-the-kid/

**Supporting project edition:**
https://www.ethiopianenglishreaders.com/16-stories/amhara/7-the-cub-and-the-leopard

The second page is a rewritten reader from the same collecting project, not
independent provenance.

**Rights:** Retell the traditional motif in original words. Do not copy either
project version or its illustrations.

**Age-fit screen:** Suitable mainly for ages 5-6 after careful softening. The
recorded conflict involves a parent encouraging predation and a false-friendly
approach. Keep danger implied, let the kid leave safely, avoid shame/name-
calling, and end with emotional security. Human reviewers should decide whether
the lesson is parent guidance, recognising unsafe behaviour, or the sadness of
natural conflict; the adaptation should not mix all three.

**Recommendation:** Advance to `Researched` with a high editorial-risk flag.
Replace it before drafting if reviewers find the trust message too frightening.

## 3. The Rat King's Son

**Collected origin:** Oromia. Narrated by Merga Debelo; the Ethiopian English
Readers page credits the Oromia Education Bureau and named translators.

**Primary project record:**
https://ethiopianfolktales.com/stories/the-rat-king-s-son/

**Licensed derivative edition:** African Storybook Initiative, 2016,
adapted/illustrated under Creative Commons Attribution 4.0:
https://www.africanstorybook.org/newviewer/index.php?id=17648

This confirms a separately licensed publication path but derives from the same
collected story and is not independent evidence of origin.

**Rights:** Strongest pilot rights position. We may make a new adaptation from
the traditional account, or adapt the African Storybook edition while retaining
its required author, illustrator, source, and CC BY 4.0 attribution. New Teret
Bet art should not imitate either source's illustrations.

**Age-fit screen:** Suitable for ages 5-6. The repeating Creator -> fog -> wind
-> mountain -> bush-rat chain supports participation and flexible pagination.
Soften contemptuous descriptions and keep marriage as a simple fairy-tale
premise rather than a lesson about status. The final theme is humility and
recognising worth close to home.

**Recommendation:** Advance to `Researched`; strongest Ethiopian pilot candidate.

## 4. The Lion and the Mouse

**Origin:** Aesopic fable with a long public-domain record.

**Primary references:**

- Library of Congress: https://read.gov/aesop/007
- Project Gutenberg, Joseph Jacobs edition:
  https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/28

**Rights:** The traditional fable and cited editions are public-domain sources
in the United States. Create new Teret Bet wording and art; do not borrow from a
modern picture-book adaptation.

**Age-fit screen:** Suitable for ages 3-6. The existing branch/net danger is
already softened. The English is currently much thinner than the Amharic and
both versions need a full natural read-aloud review. Page count must grow if the
friendship, problem, rescue, or resolution feels compressed.

**Recommendation:** Advance to `Researched` as an existing-story repair.

## 5. Stone Soup

**Origin:** European and international folktale family, classified as ATU 1548,
with multiple early variants.

**Folklore reference:**
https://sites.pitt.edu/~dash/type1548.html

**Rights:** Use the traditional plot and an early/public-domain variant as
research. Do not copy a modern children's book, its distinctive characters,
setting, wording, or art.

**Age-fit screen:** Suitable for ages 3-6 when framed as a playful invitation to
share rather than a stranger deceiving or shaming villagers. The existing text
has a workable community arc but the English is abbreviated and both languages
need independent read-aloud approval. Add pages if individual contributions or
the community's emotional change need more room.

**Recommendation:** Advance to `Researched` as an existing-story repair.

## Pilot recommendation

Keep all five for the next approval checkpoint. Draft in this order:

1. The Rat King's Son — strongest provenance/licence and repeated structure.
2. The Mouse Deer Who Was Cold at Noon — shortest low-danger concept.
3. The Lion and the Mouse — establishes repair comparison.
4. Stone Soup — tests ensemble continuity.
5. The Cub and the Kid — draft last because it needs the most editorial care.

Before drafting, the owner should approve the three Ethiopian selections and
the provisional replacements. Cultural reviewers are still required before
release, and The Cub and the Kid may be replaced without disrupting the other
four.
