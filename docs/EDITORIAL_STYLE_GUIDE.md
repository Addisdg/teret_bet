# Editorial Style Guide

## Voice

Write for a caring adult reading aloud to a child. The voice is warm, spoken,
clear, rhythmic, and respectful. Simplicity must not become mechanical or
babyish.

Amharic accuracy is the first editorial priority, followed by story continuity
and age fit, illustration consistency, and future audio readiness.

## Bilingual adaptation

- Draft and review Amharic and English as complete natural tellings.
- Preserve the same plot, character intent, page beat, and emotional arc while
  allowing language-specific rhythm and phrasing.
- Do not use English as a gloss or shortened summary of the Amharic page.
- Keep names, repeated phrases, cultural terms, and pronouns consistent.
- Explain a culturally specific word only when context cannot carry it.

## Page flow

- Page count follows the story; never pad or compress to reach a number.
- Give setup, transitions, discoveries, emotional reactions, and resolution
  enough space to breathe.
- Keep one main beat and usually 1-3 read-aloud sentences per page.
- End pages with natural forward motion, not repeated mini-endings.
- Read the complete story aloud after any page insertion or reorder.
- Add or replace artwork when the revised beat is not accurately illustrated.

## Safety and cultural care

- Soften violence, abandonment, punishment, predation, and fear without hiding
  the story's meaning.
- Prefer repair, reunion, learning, and emotionally safe closure.
- Avoid shame, name-calling, stereotypes, body jokes, and moral lectures.
- Name origin precisely. Do not label a regional tradition as universally
  Ethiopian.
- Culturally specific stories require review by someone familiar with that
  tradition before release.

## Approval evidence

Record reviewer, date, decision, and actionable notes for Amharic, English,
read-aloud, age fit, cultural care, and page flow. A text becomes `Text locked`
only after the owner explicitly approves it.
